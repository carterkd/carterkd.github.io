Review of Financial Studies · Lead article & editor’s choice

The Elasticity of Quantitative Investment

Modern cross-sectional asset-pricing models exhibit strikingly inelastic demand. A quantitative equilibrium model shows why aggregate demand remains inelastic even when statistical arbitrageurs enter the market.

Journal of Financial Economics · With Mahyar Kargar and Jiacui Li

Why Do Portfolio Choice Models Predict Inelastic Demand?

A decomposition of investor demand elasticity into price pass-through and unspanned returns helps reconcile the large gap between classical theory and empirical estimates.

Background

Research and teaching at the intersection of finance and AI.

Before joining Ohio State, I was an Assistant Professor of Finance at Indiana University’s Kelley School of Business. I earned my PhD in Finance from the University of Chicago Booth School of Business.

At Fisher, I teach Entrepreneurial Finance and Shark Tank Finance, bringing questions about venture capital, investment, and market structure into the classroom.
