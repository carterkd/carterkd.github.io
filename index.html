Research focus

How do complexity and inelastic demand shape prices?

01

Complexity prevents consensus

In high-dimensional markets, data cannot pin down every expected return. Models trained on the same information can therefore identify different profitable trades—and fail to price one another.

02

Stocks are weak substitutes

Investors place less weight on covariance risk than classical models assume. Expected-return differences can therefore spread across many weak factors, producing a factor zoo without extreme Sharpe ratios.

03

Flows move prices

When demand is inelastic, investment flows move prices.

The Elasticity of Quantitative Investment

Background

Research and teaching on how portfolio decisions become market outcomes.

I earned my PhD in Finance from the University of Chicago Booth School of Business and began my faculty career at Indiana University’s Kelley School of Business. I am now an Assistant Professor of Finance at Ohio State’s Fisher College of Business.

I usually teach FinTech courses, including Cryptoassets for undergraduates and machine learning for PhD students. I currently teach Entrepreneurial Finance at the undergraduate and graduate levels and have also taught Investments.
